Whose Words Bind

The pioneer asked how the standard had changed in a week. A traced and second-read answer showed four things. The pioneer’s own decisions wrote two lines. The rulings drawn from their corrections bind largely in the agent’s words. A check that enforces one of those rulings passes by never matching a line. And the instrument that should show the pioneer’s role changing shows it has not begun to.

base-building-kit 0.31 (e29cac2) Records from 2026-09-19 to 2026-09-26 · 3 review batches · 4 upgrades · 33 corrections 2026-09-26
2lines of skill text written by the pioneer’s own review decisions
11active precedents whose holding is wholly or largely agent-authored text
1precedent check that exits 0 on this checkout whatever the log says
0of 11 drift entries ever moved from open
32/33corrections graded redirected; none reviewed-after or did-it

The machinery works; the material it is fed is the problem. Precedents bind, the sealed key and reveal held across three batches, and the blocked state carried five corrections through a closed ledger and cleared them the same evening. Where a precedent’s holding is the pioneer’s own sentence, it steered the next contract correctly. Everything below is about what the standard is made of. In several places the record carries the agent’s reading where it claims to carry the pioneer’s, and nothing in the kit tells the two apart.

How the question was answered

The pioneer asked: “can you tell me about how we have changed the standard the last days”. The agent sent five readers through the project’s records, one per kind: candidates and batches; the casebook; the correction log; the map, mechanisms and upgrades; and the reports and drift log. A second reader checked each claim against the files. The agent then re-ran the central findings itself (see Provenance).

F-0Corrected

37 of 149 claims were wrong on first reading, and many errors came from the agent’s own summaries

The second readers also added 26 items the first readers had missed. Many of the corrected claims were not misreadings of the records. They were faithful readings of summaries the agent had earlier written into durable places: commit messages, drift entries and reports. So the durable record had inherited the error. Examples:

  • “The first audit this project has completed” is in a commit message and in a drift entry. The ledger holds a complete audit two days earlier.
  • The same presentation failure is counted two incompatible ways. The correction log and casebook call three of its recurrences the fourth, fifth and sixth. Report-007 calls the same three “the sixth, seventh and eighth occurrences of the same failure in six days”.
  • “Four held on the certainty ruling” in a commit message was three. The fourth hold was the pioneer’s own separate choice on that item.
  • Two records drafted from one correction disagree on its facts. One says seven guardrails and ten tests were left undone; the other says six of twelve guardrails were done and two of thirteen tests written.

The kit already asks that every contract be verified from outside. Nothing asks the same of the prose the agent writes into records: a count or a “first” in a commit message becomes a fact the next reader relies on.

The rulings speak in the agent’s voice

F-1Defect

Eleven active precedents take their holding from a field the agent filled in

A correction entry carries three incident fields: noticed, would_have_been_right and seen_before. They exist to hold the pioneer’s own answers, verbatim, or not asked. In practice the agent recorded each correction in the same turn and often filled would_have_been_right with its own reading of the lesson, in the third person. The case clerk then took that field as the holding. The only authorship record is recorded_by: main-agent, which names who wrote the entry, not who authored each answer.

What the pioneer saidWhat the precedent now holds (from the agent-filled field)Match
“none of that means anything”Say what each thing is in plain words — six observations not yet merged into candidates, a contract never independently verified…identical
“stay true to the skill”Run the batch exactly as meta-skill-builder prescribes — the file’s order, statement before disposition… and do not reorder…identical
“i have no idea what that means”Say what the housekeeping job is, why it cannot happen now, how long it waits… with no kit t[erms]…identical
“you have lost direction here, that is why you ask me” (noticed: not asked)Read the bearing against the founding statement, which already decides it…0.89

By the same comparison, nine active precedents are identical to that field. Two more are 0.89 and 0.76 similar. One further precedent is identical but already overruled. By contrast, two precedents whose field holds the pioneer’s own sentence show the field working as designed. One reads “You should have proposed solutions and waited for my input”. The other is the most recent, where the pioneer answered all three questions themselves.

The case where it binds the wrong way. The last row above became a precedent holding that the founding statement already decides such questions. When the pioneer later ranked the card for that same moment, their reason was:

B, you were lost in the woods and were asking what tree was the way out, but none were. The solution was to leave that context behind and think broader, read the roadmap and the code and look with new eyes.

The binding precedent and the pioneer’s own reason point in opposite directions. The precedent is the agent’s inference, recorded where an answer was never asked for. No conflict is marked, because the kit compares precedents with precedents, not with card rationales.

This is the most consequential finding here. Since 0.25, precedents bind, and only the pioneer can overrule one. A binding rule that is the agent’s own words, and that no one presents to the pioneer, is exactly the loophole this project’s founding statement exists to exclude, one layer up.

F-2Defect

A binding ruling did not stop the same failure a day later; an outside reading did

The newest precedent is the pioneer’s own. At an approval gate the agent offered narrower tests, and the pioneer answered “testing is central” and broadened instead. The next contract cited that precedent in its ground and promised to run the earlier contract’s end-to-end tests “exactly as written”. Its first draw ran two of the eight refusals those tests name. The agent was not the one who caught it. Five independent challengers attacked the draft, four of them found it, and a second reader confirmed it. The contract was redrawn before the gate.

The pioneer asked for that challenge on the previous contract (“I want you to challenge the contract, what does not hold its claim?”). At the spec lock of this one they asked for the same after the build: “after the contract run adversarial agents and fix bugs and issues and rerun agents until its resolved.” The kit has no such step. The contract procedure ends at the agent’s own draw and the pioneer’s gate.

Checks and precedents that do not hold themselves

F-3Defect

The only precedent check passes by never matching a line

checks/P-010.sh enforces “finish the approved contract before proposing work beyond it”. It reads CONTRACT-LOG.yaml line by line and matches " status: approved" exactly. On this Windows checkout the log has CRLF endings (3,255 carriage returns), so every line ends \r, no line ever matches, and the script exits 0. Today three contracts are open at once and it still exits 0. The casebook records the check as passes.

The CRLF did not come from git: the file carries eol=lf. It came from the agent’s own edits. Scripts writing in text mode on Windows convert line endings silently. Any kit check that compares whole lines is exposed the same way on any Windows project whose records the agent edits with ordinary tools.

$ bash .claude/skills/meta-mechanisms/checks/P-010.sh; echo "exit=$?"
exit=0          # three contracts are status approved / implemented

Afterwards. While logging this report, the agent wrote the contract log back with LF endings. The same check now exits 1: P-010 broken: more than one contract left open at once — contract-005 contract-004 contract-003. So the check was sound all along. Whether it can see anything depended on how the last tool to touch the file wrote its line endings, and no hook runs it on its own.

The kit’s own principle is that a test is seen to fail before it is trusted. The red run was done when the check was written, on a file that then had LF endings. Nothing re-proves a check on the machine it runs on, so “passes” and “cannot fail here” look the same.

F-4Gap

An authorised departure from a precedent leaves no trace

The three open contracts are not drift. The pioneer chose it: “run both”, keeping one contract open while the next was drawn and approved. But the precedent’s distinguished_by is empty, and nothing prompts it. The approval moment runs no precedent check, so a departure the pioneer authorised looks the same as one nobody noticed. With the check silent (F-3), there is no signal at all.

F-5Gap

A ranked card can contradict an active precedent, unmarked

In the second batch the pioneer ranked a card [B, A]. B was their own earlier instruction, “The agent should present candidates that scores high on reliability, those it feels are strong or likely to be adopted.” That instruction had become a precedent, and their “stay true to the skill” overruled it minutes later. The overruling precedent (follow the file’s order) is still active. So the casebook now holds a ranked card and an active precedent that lean opposite ways on the same moment, and no conflict: marker exists. The reason given with the ranking says the fix belongs upstream: “Not sure this should be declined, but it should be fixed in the meta kit repo”.

The pioneer’s instructions about the practice have no home

F-6Pioneer

A ruling on what reviews show was given, and not applied to the very next batch

In the first batch the pioneer ruled:

Id like to only rule on items that has cetainty high or higher / i rule held on everyone that isnt high or higher

The second batch, assembled the same day, put a candidate rated moderate to them, and they decided it. In that batch they restated the rule more sharply:

The order is not important as long as only the candidates and cards with HIGH certainty are the only ones showed.

The rule lives nowhere the assembler reads. It lives only in batch reasons, in the agent’s private memory and in a held note. The kit has no record kind for a pioneer’s standing instruction about the practice itself, as distinct from a correction of the work. Two further instructions of that kind are in the same state:

  • The presentation forms. “Draw a form for candidates and one for cards that you will use and enforce from now on.” This was never entered as a correction. The forms lived only in conversation, were lost at the first compaction, were rebuilt wrongly from the batch file’s layout for the next batch, and survive now only in the agent’s memory file.
  • “Yes, but upstream.” “oh, that will be handled by the next version of the meta kit” was recorded as decline, because the vocabulary has no exit for “accepted, to be fixed in the kit”.

And the rule cannot be applied as given: cards carry no certainty (report-007 F-3, again). This week’s only card reached the pioneer with a note saying so.

F-7Pioneer

“Would it be enforced or remembered?”

The one line the pioneer’s review wrote into INTENT.md reads: what is waiting on the pioneer is put to them as a decision when it accumulates, never left as a count in a status line for them to ask about. Before deciding, they asked whether it would be enforced or remembered. Checked against 0.31:

  • Enforced: the session-start hook tells the agent to run waiting-on-you.sh and put the list first.
  • Remembered: whether the agent does, and whether what it writes is readable.
  • The gap: the hook fires at session start only. The backlog that prompted the correction grew across one long session, to fifty-one candidates.

The pioneer’s framing is the feedback. A line in the always-loaded file is remembered, and a hook is enforced. The kit should say which each line is, because a line that reads as a rule and is carried only by memory fails exactly when the session is long enough to matter. Every instruction in F-6 is currently remembered, and one of them has already been lost.

Records that stopped moving

F-8Gap

Repeated corrections are filed as new candidates, so recurrence is invisible to the ledger

The pioneer corrected one failure six times in three days: the agent presenting in the kit’s own vocabulary. They said “none of that means anything”, “Again, i dont know that t-2, t12,G12 or any of the other ids mean”, “you need to work harder to present it to me in a clear structured and accessible way”, “i have no idea what that means”, “It was very hard to understand” and “you regressed in how you presented now”. The consolidator filed six separate candidates. Each has restatements: 1 and certainty high, and none is due. To the certainty machinery, the pioneer’s most repeated correction looks like six single sightings.

F-9Gap

The drift log has not changed since the 20th

Eleven entries, all open, none ever moved to watching or resolved. That includes entries whose fix landed by contract, such as a declared proxy the server did not trust and a certificate setting nothing read. The presentation failure above recurred three more times after the entry that tracks it was last updated. Its recurrence_count still reads 2. Nothing prompts the agent to move, link or bump an entry, and it did none of these.

F-10Gap

Bookkeeping the adoption and upgrade paths leave undone

  • The first adoption changed no manifest entry. The sentence went into INTENT.md. The M-22 route says the manifest is updated in the same act, and the node still reads thin with no record of it.
  • Base node lines were never refreshed across four upgrades. The manifest still describes base-antidrift as post-output drift scoring, which 0.29 replaced with the closing four.
  • 0.27 reworded six map rows that stay marked ratified, including one that dropped “or debugging” from a trigger. No re-ratification of the new wording is recorded.
  • Every report in the contract log reads led_to: null, including reports whose asks later upgrades plainly took up.
  • One rehearsal finding went nowhere. The 0.31 rehearsal found the written upgrade list omits registering a new base node (only the migration check surfaced it). That finding is in no report.
F-11Measured

The fading curve has not begun, and part of that is the instrument

Of 33 corrections, 32 are graded redirected and one hinted. None is reviewed-after or did-it, and there are zero bearing re-evaluations. On its face, the pioneer’s judgement is not yet carried by the standard. Two things in the instrument make that reading unsafe:

  • An override is not an error, and the log cannot tell them apart (report-007 recommendation 6). Twice this week the pioneer ranked the agent’s original response above their own correction: “THe reason is i understand when i want you to push ahead, but you dont. Its my point to hold.”
  • Corrections given while a contract is being drawn carry no contract. The newest correction was given at a spec lock, before the contract it shaped had an approved entry, so its contract_id is null. The curve counts it under “no contract”. Every spec-lock correction will be miscounted the same way.

No mechanism shows the curve to the pioneer. The agent reported it for the first time this week, because they asked a question that happened to need it.

Recommendation — judgement, not finding

  1. Record who authored each answer in a correction, and do not let agent-authored text bind unmarked (F-1). Give the three incident fields an author: pioneer, agent or not asked. When the clerk takes a holding from agent-authored text, it should mark holding_source: agent-inferred. Such a precedent should not bind until the pioneer has kept it at a batch; the precedent kind already offers keep/overrule. The cheapest version is a check that fails when a holding equals an incident field whose author is not the pioneer.
  2. Make every check prove it can fail where it runs (F-3). Strip \r in every line-matching check, or parse the YAML. Ship each check with a red fixture, run the walk with a CRLF variant, and treat a check that has never failed on this machine as unknown, not passes.
  3. Run precedent checks at approval (F-4). A failing check at M-06 requires either a distinguished_by line in the pioneer’s words or a pioneer overrule before the approval is recorded.
  4. Mark conflicts at reveal (F-5, F-1). When a ranked card’s winning option, or its rationale, contradicts an active precedent from the same moment, write conflict: and put it to the pioneer at the next batch.
  5. Give the pioneer’s instructions about the practice a record every session reads (F-6, F-7). This covers review scope (minimum certainty), the item forms, and an exit for “accepted, fix upstream”. For every line in the always-loaded file, say whether it is enforced or remembered. The assembler should read the review policy, and cards need a certainty or an explicit exemption.
  6. Add an independent challenge of the draft before the gate (F-2). This pioneer has asked for it at every contract since they saw it work. The binding precedent did not prevent the narrowing it was about; the second reading did.
  7. Count restatements across candidates (F-8), by moment family and target, so that a correction repeated six times reads as one claim seen six times.
  8. Keep drift moving (F-9). The clerk bumps recurrence_count when a correction repeats an open entry’s pattern, and verification offers the link when a contract’s clause fixes an entry.
  9. Put the undone bookkeeping on the upgrade sheet (F-10). Refresh base manifest lines. Mark reworded ratified rows for re-ratification. Ask which reports the release answers and back-fill led_to.
  10. Let a spec-lock correction carry the contract being drawn (F-11), and show the curve at review batches.
  11. Hold the agent’s durable prose to the evidence standard (F-0). A count or a “first” written into a commit message, drift entry or report should quote its record, or be checked by a second reading, as the verifier checks a contract.

What should not change. Precedents should keep binding. The defect is in what some of them are made of, not in the binding. Also keep statement-first review, the sealed key and the blocked state, all of which worked this week under a pioneer who reads every word. And where a precedent is the pioneer’s own sentence, it did its job: it put “testing is central” into the ground of the next contract.
