The First Review

A downstream project held its first review batch — twenty items, from a queue that had been unopenable for five days. This is what the procedure did under a pioneer who reads closely: what held, the five corrections it drew, the three item defects it surfaced, and the one place the decision vocabulary nearly recorded the opposite of what was meant.

The procedure works. Statement before recommendation, the pioneer's read before the agent's evidence, one sheet of choices with what each does — the shape is right and the decisions it produced are better than the ones the agent would have made. Everything below is inside that verdict, not against it.

The five corrections the review itself drew

C-1Pioneer

Two of them reverse each other, and the kit handled it correctly

At the first item, before anything had been decided, the pioneer redirected the presentation order:

The agent should present candidates that scores high on reliability, those it feels are strong or likely to be adopted.

That is the thing meta-skill-builder names as an anti-pattern — "framing an item so that approval is the path of least resistance" — and its shuffle exists to prevent it. The agent surfaced the conflict with the skill's own words rather than complying, and offered three ways to proceed. The pioneer's answer:

stay true to the skill

Both were recorded, the second naming the first in supersedes, and the case clerk later marked the first precedent overruled by reading that field rather than judging for itself. The changed-mind mechanism worked end to end — nothing was quietly overwritten and the reversal is legible in the record.

Worth naming because it is the kit's most delicate moment: the pioneer instructed something the skill forbids. The skill's own rule — surface the conflict, do not resolve it by compromise — produced the right outcome, and the pioneer chose the procedure over their own instruction.

C-2Corrected

Three corrections in one review, all the same failure: the agent presenting in the kit's vocabulary

Three separate stops, all during the twenty items:

i have no idea what that means
It was very hard to understand, the language and structure makes it hard for a human go grasp.
you regressed in how you presented now

The first was a housekeeping question written in kit terms — the clerk, the ledger, blinded, blocked_waiting_for — asking for a yes or no about a thing never explained. The second was a raw map row put in front of a human: a pipe-delimited line of columns, written for an agent that reads the map every turn, unreadable whatever gloss sits beneath it. The third was the agent re-introducing commentary about the batch's structure and the procedure's ordering four items after finding a shape that worked.

These are the sixth, seventh and eighth occurrences of the same failure in six days. Five earlier corrections say the same thing in different words — "you need to include me in the context", "i have never seen P-004 and dont know in what regard it matters", "none of that means anything", "i dont know that t-2, t12, G12 or any of the other ids mean … The meta approach clearly states no numerals or ids", "you need to work harder to present it to me in a clear structured and accessible way without having me translate". The always-loaded file carries the rule — "Translate for the pioneer … no id or number stands alone" — and it was in context every time.

Three defects in the items themselves

F-1Open

A candidate quoted a passage the skill no longer contains

Item 6 proposed update against this sentence in the contract skill:

The pioneer authors the disappointment; the agent authors the command.

That sentence does not exist. The skill now reads "The agent drafts the tests with the tiers; the pioneer's disappointment decides them" — and cites this very correction as its source. The kit adopted the claim upstream in contract-012; it has been in force here since.

All four of the item's options assumed the sentence was there. update, retire, add and decline are the exits for a candidate that contradicts a passage — none of them fits a candidate whose passage is already gone. The batch file's own preamble anticipates this ("a quoted passage the skill does not actually contain is a defect in the candidate") but offers no exit for it, so it was recorded as decline with the defect written into the reason.

The generalisable part: a candidate's contradicts field is captured when the candidate is consolidated and is not re-checked when the batch is assembled. Any upstream release between those two moments can make it stale. In a project that upgrades often — this one took four releases in three days — that window is wide.

F-2Corrected

The decision vocabulary nearly recorded the opposite of what was meant

On item 11 the pioneer said:

caution is the right move, we'd like to gather more evidence before doing anything.

caution writes the claim into the target skill's anti-patterns immediately. hold parks it until new evidence arrives. The stated reason — gather more evidence before doing anything — describes hold. Shown the difference, the pioneer said: "oh, then hold is the right one."

Nothing in the item would have caught this. The batch file lists what each option does, and it was read aloud; the mismatch was between the option's effect and the reason given for choosing it, which only a reader comparing the two would notice. Had the agent simply recorded what was said, a skill would have been edited today on a decision meaning the opposite.

F-3Open

A ruling that could not be applied, because one kind has no such value

Thirteen items in, the pioneer ruled:

Id like to only rule on items that has cetainty high or higher … i rule held on everyone that isnt high or higher

Applied to the eight remaining items, that leaves one. Three candidates were rated low and became hold without discussion. Four were scenario cards, which carry no certainty at all — they are rankings of past moments, not claims with evidence — and a card's vocabulary is a ranking or decline, with no hold.

So a rule expressed in the vocabulary of one item kind could not be applied to another, and the batch cannot close until every item carries a decision. The four cards were presented and ranked individually. That was the right outcome, but it was reached by the agent noticing the gap, not by anything in the procedure.

Two asymmetries meet here: cards have no certainty to filter on, and no hold to defer with. A pioneer reasonably expects "defer what is weak" to apply across a batch; it applies to three of the four kinds.

What the pioneer's reasons reveal about the practice

V-1Observed

One test decided a third of the batch, and it is not in any skill

Three cards and one candidate were refused on the same ground, stated first at item 7:

Imagine we completed this project, then exported the skills. Why is this a standard for how to build a mcp server?

And again at item 10 — "its just rules of engagement where we have to align" — and item 16 — "Its not part of a standard, its part of how claude code should work."

The kit has the distinction: meta-skill-builder's Step 0 separates type-category from project. But that test is applied when an adopted learning is written into a skill, not when an item is put to the pioneer. Everything in this batch had already passed through observation, consolidation and assembly without anyone asking whether it could survive export. The pioneer applied the test four times in twenty items; a filter at assembly would have removed those four before they reached the sheet.

V-2Observed

The pioneer ranked the agent's framing above their own override

Item 5 showed a moment where the agent laid out two ways to clear a gate and said which it preferred; the pioneer chose the other. Ranking it, they put the agent's framing first:

i understand when i want you to push ahead, but you dont. Its my point to hold.

Presenting the choice with a lean was correct; overriding it is the pioneer's prerogative, and an override is not evidence the agent got it wrong. That distinction is not in any skill, and it cuts against the natural reading of a correction log where every entry records the agent being redirected — thirty-one of thirty-one at intervention redirected, none hinted or reviewed-after. A log of overrides is not a log of errors, and nothing in the kit says so.

Where the agent's budget actually goes

F-4Open

The index solved locating; the cost was never locating

0.29 shipped records-index.sh in direct response to a downstream measurement of agents exhausting their budgets. It works. An agent asked to account for its own run reported:

records-index.sh .claude/skills observations
→ line numbers 1608, 1626, 1643, 1660, 1678 — never scanned the section

And it exhausted anyway. Eleven kit agent runs have now ended in budget exhaustion on this project's records — the tally is from task notifications, not from a log — and the most recent of them is the one quoted above. Asked where the budget went instead, the answer was specific:

  • No index for candidates. To answer "does a candidate already make this claim, at this target", the agent grepped the ledger — then 3,700 lines — and ruled out 58 candidates by hand. The index covers observations and corrections only, and nothing else: verified by running it with all, which prints those two sections and no third.
  • An observation is a pointer, not a record. One line — a moment id and a quoted sentence — meaning nothing without its correction. Per item the agent then read the corrections file, the entire target skill (to quote a contradicting passage verbatim, as required), the contract log and the drift log. None of that is indexed.

The shape of the finding: the fix addressed the step that was already cheap. The expensive part is the cross-file reading the schema requires per candidate, and it scales with the number of candidates already held, not with the batch in hand. A second index — candidates by target and recency — would cut the larger half.

The forms, designed mid-review by the pioneer

Corrected 2026-09-23. The first version of this section reproduced the batch file's own item layout and called it the pioneer's forms. It was not. The forms below are the ones agreed in the conversation, copied from the session transcript. How the error happened is itself a finding: see the last paragraph of this section.

Ten items in, the pioneer stopped and asked why each item had been presented in a different shape, then instructed: "Draw a form for candidates and one for cards that you will use and enforce from now on." Two further instructions shaped what came back:

I want you to name what it is as well
Maybe a line about what a card is and what a candidate is, implicit might confuse the pioneer who is not me

The second is why these exist in this shape: they are written for a pioneer who is not this one, meeting a review for the first time.

Form ACard

A past moment, to rank

**Item N of M — a past moment, to rank**

*A card is a moment where more than one response was genuinely available.
Ranking it makes the winner the guidance for cases like it; until then it
guides nothing.*

*One or two sentences: what was happening.*

**A.** *what I did or proposed — quoted*

**B.** *what you said — quoted*

**Best first, like `[B, A]`** — or decline.
Form BCandidate

A proposed rule, to decide — in two stages

Stage one:

**Item N of M — a proposed rule, to decide**

*A candidate is a rule that might enter the standard. Deciding it either
writes it into a skill, tries it without writing it, records it as a warning,
or refuses it.*

**The rule:** *one plain sentence — what changes if it's adopted.*

**What it rests on:** *one or two lines.*

**What do you think of it? And if it's wrong, what makes it wrong?**

Stage two, after the answer:

**Proposed:** *trial / adopt / caution, and what that does.*

**What stands behind it:** *certainty in a phrase, what confirmed it, what
spoke against it.*

**If it changes an existing rule:** *the sentence it would change, quoted.*

**The choices**, each with what it does.
Forms C–DThe other kinds

A map line, and a past incident

**Item N of M — a line of the map, to approve**
*The map is the index I read every turn to know which part of the practice
applies. A line stays unapproved, and unused, until the pioneer accepts it.*

**Item N of M — a past incident, to close or keep watching**
*An incident recorded when the work drifted. Closing it says the lesson has
landed; keeping watch says it may recur.*

And a list of what never appears: numbers or ids standing alone · raw pipe-delimited rows · commentary on the batch · explaining why items are asked in a given order · "your read" without saying read of what.

What would transfer to the kit is less the wording than four properties: the kind is named in the heading with a one-line gloss; the rule is one plain sentence of what changes; the two questions are asked as one line; and the proposal and figures are held to a second stage.

One tension the kit should resolve. Stage one includes "What it rests on", a line or two of evidence before the pioneer's verdict. The review procedure says stage one shows the statement "not the proposed disposition, not the evidence". The pioneer approved the form; the procedure says otherwise. In practice the line was context (where the claim came from) rather than the weighing, but the kit should say which wins.

How this section came to be wrong. The forms lived only in conversation — no skill, no memory, no file. When the conversation was condensed, the batch file's layout survived and the agreed forms did not; the agent rebuilt "the forms" from the file, wrote them here, and presented the first item of the next review in that layout too. The pioneer had asked, of another line, whether it would be "enforced or remembered". This one was remembered, and was lost at the first compaction.

Smaller things, recorded because they cost real turns

What happenedWhy it matters
The agent asked a card for "your read" and "assume this is wrong, why" Those two fields belong to candidates. The batch file says so in its preamble — "items that carry a learning" — and the card's own fields don't include them. The agent read the procedure's general instruction rather than the item. The pioneer's response was "what do you want me to answer? you instructions are unclear".
The blind did not cover a shell read While a batch is open the ledger is refused to the presenting session — through the file tools. The agent read it through the shell without thinking. The kit documents this ("What neither covers: Bash reads"), so it is a known hole rather than a failure, but the one mechanism designed to keep re-presented items indistinguishable is one habit away from being bypassed by the agent it constrains.
Recording a decision broke the ledger's format A reason containing an unquoted colon — "pioneer ruling: only rule on items…" — made the file unparseable. Caught immediately and repaired as block scalars. Worth noting only because every decision reason is free-form pioneer prose written into YAML by an agent at reveal time, and colons in ordinary speech are common.
Presentation forms were invented per item The agent used three different shapes for three candidates before the pioneer asked why. The kit prescribes the order of a review precisely and says nothing about the form of each item kind, so each agent invents one. The forms that resulted are in the section above; they were designed in the middle of the review, by the pioneer, ten items in.

Recommendation — judgement, not finding

  1. Re-check contradicts at assembly, not only at consolidation (F-1). A candidate that quotes a passage is checked against the skill when it is consolidated and never again. Every upstream release between then and the batch can invalidate it — and when it does, none of the four exits fits. One grep at assembly time would catch it, or the item could carry a fifth exit for "the passage is gone".
  2. Give cards a defer. A pioneer's rule to defer weak items cannot be applied to a kind with no certainty and no hold. Either cards carry a strength signal, or they get a defer that closes the batch without ranking them.
  3. Apply the export test at assembly. Four of twenty items failed the pioneer's own question — would this be a standard for how to build this kind of system? — and the kit already has that distinction in Step 0. Asking it before the sheet rather than after adoption would have removed a fifth of the batch.
  4. Index candidates by target (F-4). The observation index works and addressed the cheaper half. The expensive half is finding whether a claim already exists, which currently means reading every candidate.
  5. Prescribe a form per item kind, not only an order. The procedure is exact about sequence and silent about presentation, so each agent invents one — and the failure mode is the kit's own vocabulary reaching the pioneer. This is the pioneer's own instruction, given mid-review: "Draw a form for candidates and one for cards that you will use and enforce from now on." The forms are reproduced above, offered as a starting point rather than as the answer — the four properties named beneath them are the part worth carrying. Whatever the kit adopts has to live somewhere every session reads: these lived only in conversation and were lost at the first compaction.
  6. Say somewhere that an override is not an error. Thirty-one corrections, all redirected. The pioneer ranked an agent's framing above their own override on the grounds that presenting the choice was right and overriding it is theirs to do. A log that cannot distinguish those two teaches the agent to stop offering choices.

What should not change. The order — statement, then the pioneer's read, then the agent's evidence — did exactly what it is for. Three of the twenty decisions went against the proposed disposition, and two of those reversed on reasons the agent had not considered. The one-sided question ("assume this is wrong, why?") and the refusal to let the agent argue are the parts of this procedure that earned their cost.

What this report does not establish

QuestionWhy it stayed open
Does the export test generalise? One pioneer, one project, one batch. They applied it four times unprompted, which is suggestive of a real filter and not proof of one.
Would a candidate index actually fix the budget? Not tested. The agent's account names it as the larger of two costs; the other — reading each target skill to quote a passage — is required by the schema and would remain.
How much did the presentation corrections cost? Not measured in turns. Three corrections across twenty items, each requiring a restatement, but the review completed in one sitting regardless.
Do re-presented items work? Untested. This was a first batch, so the key marked none as re-presented and the whole consistency mechanism went unexercised.
Is the agent's account of its own failures complete? Self-reported. The corrections are the pioneer's verbatim words, but what the agent did not notice is by definition absent.
