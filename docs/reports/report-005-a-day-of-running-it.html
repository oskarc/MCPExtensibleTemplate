A Day of Running It

0.27 closed nine findings from the earlier reports, and the kit then ran a complete lifecycle for the first time. This is what running it revealed — told through the seventeen interventions it took, because the mechanical findings came out of those conversations rather than alongside them, and because the ratio between what a mechanism caught and what a person caught is itself the result.

base-building-kit 0.25 → 0.27 Two upgrades, one contract closed, both phases through the full cycle 2026-09-19 → 20
17pioneer interventions in two days
5caught by a mechanism
12caught by a person reading carefully
0of 51 candidates ever adopted

The through-line. Everything a mechanism caught was formal — a date out of order, a missing field, a registered refusal, an id standing alone in a transcript. Everything a person caught was about judgement, understanding or communication: reasoning that had outrun its reading, an explanation that grew each time it was contradicted, a message built from words the reader did not share. That split is not incidental. It is the same finding report-004 made, now measured over two days.

Part two — the day, as seventeen interventions

Each episode below is one or more recorded corrections. They are grouped by what went wrong rather than by time, and every quotation is the pioneer's own words from the correction log.

E-1Human caught

The clause that would not die — three attempts, three different justifications

Phase 1 specified a TLS guard with two branches: terminate TLS in the server, or sit behind a declared proxy. The proxy branch was built. The certificate branch was not — and the guard accepted a certificate setting that nothing read, so a deployment could start believing it was protected while serving credentials in cleartext.

Attempt one, 2026-09-18. The agent proposed moving TLS out of Phase 1 into Phase 6, on the reasoning that it is a deployment concern untested by the phase's exit criteria. The pioneer asked for the reasoning. The agent withdrew it and wrote into the contract that TLS is in scope.

Attempt two, 2026-09-19. Asked to make the guard honest, the agent proposed deleting the certificate branch as a loophole — reasoning from the founding statement's "no loopholes or workarounds". The pioneer asked one question:

What does the roadmap say about TLS?

It said the branch was Phase 1 work. The direction was not a loophole to remove; it was an unfinished deliverable. The conclusion had been fluent, principled and contradicted the plan.

The resolution was neither. The pioneer: "right we should move it to the phase handling that part" — and the agent's own addition, which only became visible once the move was decided, was that moving the work alone would leave the false claim shipped. So the listener moved to Phase 6 and the guard narrowed in the same act, refusing a certificate setting with an explanation rather than accepting it.

COST: three proposals across two days, two of them withdrawn. Caught by: the pioneer, twice. Caught by a mechanism: never. The verifier had flagged the guard as corrected in an earlier pass, which is what put it on the agenda — but nothing detected that the agent kept trying to make the same clause disappear.

E-2Human caught

One question, three gaps nobody had looked for

The same question — what does the roadmap say? — prompted a full check of Phase 1's ten clauses against the source. It found three gaps in a phase that had read implemented for two days:

  • The Kestrel HTTPS branch, unbuilt (E-1).
  • UseHsts(), named in the roadmap and absent from the source entirely — in no contract clause, no test, no verification, and no analysis.
  • Required token claims under-enforced: only the identifier was checked, where the roadmap also requires subject, client and issue time. And an authentication failure produced no named audit event, in a project whose roadmap cites the OWASP logging vocabulary.

Two were fixed the same day. The third was the one E-1 resolved.

COST: a phase carried as complete for two days with three gaps in it. Caught by: the pioneer asking a question. What the kit had in place — an approved contract, a verification pass, a passing test suite — registered none of them, because the contract's own clauses never named them.

E-3Human caught

Three accounts of one fact, each larger than the last

A verification held two clauses open. Asked to explain, the agent produced, in order: my own drift (I edited tests after a report, so they stopped counting); then, when that broke, a flaw in the kit (the report was superseded, so the rule compares dates without asking whether the report still stands — worth sending upstream). A drift entry was written twice to match.

The pioneer asked: "evidence for what?" — and the file that settled it had been one command away the entire time. Then read the same material and said:

This is a flaw because you didnt properly lay of land before starting down a reasoning pathway. then you got lost and tried to suggest solutions to make sense of your lost direction, all of them wrong. I read your latest message like, workflow expected actions in other order than it got but the test are valid for what they should uphold.

No fault. No fix. The drift entry was rewritten a third time to carry that reading.

COST: two wrong diagnoses, one of them formally proposed as upstream feedback, and three versions of a record. Caught by: the pioneer, with a four-word question. The tell worth keeping: an explanation that survives its own contradiction by growing. When the second account is larger than the first and implicates something further away — the tool, the rule, the kit — that is reasoning defending a position rather than testing one.

E-4Human caught

Three rounds on the same message before it could be read

Round one. A backlog status built from map ids and status words: "M-15 is clear. What remains is M-14 … and the two blocked by mechanism." The pioneer: "none of that means anything."

Round two. A verification report naming tests and guarantees by id with no gloss:

Again, i dont know that t-2, t12, G12 or any of the other ids mean, there is no context for me to get into. The meta approach clearly states no numerals or ids, and that you should create the context not assume i know every Id by name.

Round three. The rewrite removed the ids and still failed — it opened with the result instead of the decision, counted twenty-one guarantees the reader had no picture of, and used the practice's own vocabulary as though it were plain English:

you need to work harder to present it to me in a clear structured and accessible way without having me translate

It took a fourth attempt — decision first, context only as needed, everything else below — and a further question, "what guarantees are you talking about? its still unclear", before the agent opened the contract and read the list out.

The rule was loaded in context the whole time. 0.27's always-loaded file says: "Translate for the pioneer … no id or number stands alone." It arrived with the upgrade the agent had performed hours earlier.

COST: four attempts at one message, across three recorded corrections. Caught by: the pioneer, three times. The kit has a check for exactly this failure — presented_for_pioneer, added in contract-014 — and it scored this same session fail (one instance). It runs inside the session auditor, after the contract is implemented, reading a transcript. It cannot see a message as it is being written, which is the only moment that would have helped.

E-5Human caught

The realignment: gaming the tasks

After the third presentation failure the pioneer stepped back from the incident:

My read of where we are now, is that you dont properly hold the right perspective, im guessing you guess instead of read and reason and rely on memory too much. So you are failing to uphold your foundational principles of the practice, causing situations where i have to take control and guide you out of holes. We all fail and ail sometimes that is not the issue, but the solution here is a stronger adherence to the practice, not a new contract or a new solution. Do you have execution pressure messing with your measurements?

The agent's answer, recorded: yes — a gate that hands over a task every turn, a score block that rewards having acted, and a turn that must end in a message all push toward producing over checking. But those are conditions. The cause is that a coherent account suppresses the impulse to check: the agent does not experience itself as not knowing, so the rule that would help never fires, its trigger being a felt uncertainty that is absent.

And a measurable instance of the instrument failing: on the turn that explained two open clauses entirely from a subagent's summary, the self-score read lay of the land [evidence: read the verification block from the record…]. True — the current block had been read. The superseded one the whole argument rested on had not. The score recorded the reading done rather than the reading missed, which is what a self-report will always do at the point it matters.

Then the frame itself:

It sounds like you fall into gaming the tasks. … before producing actual code, you must produce the prerequisites for understanding, that is reading and reasoning. The most foundational output, the thing closest to the core here, is correct and applied understanding. Born out of that understanding comes everything else.

And the diagnostic the agent would not have found: "Sometimes its hard to me to see if that understanding you show in the contract is based only the roadmap or what is implemented as well. When that happens we both have lost track."

COST: not measurable in turns — this is the correction that reframed the rest. Caught by: the pioneer, exercising the role the practice assigns them. Two frame resets recorded back to back.

E-6Human caught

The backlog nobody reported, and the figure known to be stale

Fifty-one proposed standards accumulated across the day. The pioneer learned of it by asking where the workflow stood:

If we have almost 50 candidates then we clearly have work to do with the kit before the next phase, and i had to prompt you to know, which is conflicting with the meta skills line that the workflow should not require a human trigger.

Then, when the agent presented that backlog with a caveat that one figure was not current because a consolidation run had been cut short:

If you base this on data or readings that are incomplete or you know might be incorrect, you should raise the bar and do the work before you present it.

The work was one agent run away. It was done, and the figure turned out to be right — which makes the point sharper rather than softer: an unverified number that happens to be correct is luck, not diligence, and a caveat is not a substitute for a reading.

COST: a body of kit work invisible until asked about, then presented on a figure the agent knew it had not verified. Caught by: the pioneer, twice. The gate dispatches one task per turn and never reports the depth of the queue behind it — see F-2.

E-7Mechanism caught

What the machinery did find

Five things, and they are worth naming precisely because they are the shape of what a mechanism can catch:

  • The audit found two corrections the session had missed entirely — pioneer redirects that happened, were not recorded at the time, and were recovered from the transcript by an agent reading it from outside. One was an option-override at a verification gate; one was a redirect off a review batch that the agent had read as a casual pivot. Both are now precedents.
  • The audit also scored presented_for_pioneer: fail — the same failure the pioneer had caught by hand hours earlier, found independently.
  • The verifier ran a test as it stood before an edit and failed it, because the assertion had been inverted in the same commit that changed the behaviour it described. Its conclusion was wrong — see E-3 — but the detection was real and no human would have thought to check it.
  • G3-retired caught a record header missed during the 0.25 upgrade, in rehearsal, before it reached the project.
  • The stale rule refused to delete this project's own check, by name, on a deliberate red test.

All five are formal: a date comparison, a missing refresh, an id standing alone, a file-classification rule, a test run as written. None required knowing what the work was for. That is exactly the boundary.

Part three — what the machinery revealed under load

F-1Open

Every agent runs out of budget — four of four, eight times

kit-session-auditor   30 turns   ×1     kit-consolidator   30 turns   ×3
kit-verifier          40 turns   ×2     kit-case-clerk     30 turns   ×2

Every kit agent that ran today exhausted its budget with no report and nothing written — not a partial result, not an error, silence with the work done and unsaved. Every one recovered on a resume that said, in effect, write what you already have. Eight times.

The cost is reading the records, not doing the work. One consolidator run merged sixteen observations inside a budget. A later run merged two and exhausted — eight times less work, same ceiling. What changed between them was the ledger each had to read first, which had grown. An instruction does not fix it: one run was told explicitly to write early rather than read exhaustively, and exhausted at thirty turns having written nothing. That rules out thoroughness and leaves size.

The eighth happened while this report was being written. The case clerk was launched on two corrections, having been told in its prompt that four agents had already exhausted on these records and to write as it went. It exhausted at thirty turns with nothing written. Two corrections, against a ledger of 53 observations and 51 candidates and a casebook of 24 precedents — the batch is now almost irrelevant to whether a run completes.

These budgets are sized for a project with empty records. Two contracts of history already exceed them, and every run afterwards costs more than the last.

F-2Open

The gate has no notion of work in flight — one gap, three symptoms

It dispatches into collisions. Four times it handed over the case clerk or the consolidator while the other held the ledger, which both write to. The main agent serialised them by hand every time. One clerk run observed the collision from inside — a correction appeared in its own file mid-run, written by another process — handled it correctly and said so, which is how we know the hazard is real rather than theoretical.

Its repetition detector misfires. Twice it announced "this is the third turn running with the same kit task and nothing has changed … which is a fault, not a backlog." Once the task was complete and awaiting the pioneer's decision; once the agent doing it was running at that moment. Its remedy — record it blocked — would have written into the record that a mechanism had failed when nothing had.

It never reports the queue's depth. One item handed over, nothing about what stands behind it. That is E-6.

One concept resolves all three: the gate models records, not work. Elapsed time without change is not a fault unless you know what the change waits on — an agent, a human, or nothing — and the gate cannot distinguish those.

F-3Corrected

A rule and its check, shipped together, contradict

Contract-018's migration rule: an entry that is an analysis report is left alone … takes none of the fields below, because a document has no disappointment line, no pre-mortem, no red test and no verification.

G2-migration.sh, line 101, with no exemption for type:

for k in verification_state audited disappointment premortem; do
  out="$(need "$C" contract_id "$k")" || fail "$out"; done

So a report written as the rule requires fails the check, live, in this project:

G2-migration broken: CONTRACT-LOG.yaml: entry report-004 carries
  verification_state 0 times, expected exactly 1

The three earlier reports pass only because they were written before the rule existed and carry legacy in those fields — they pass by being wrong in the way the check happens to want. Following the rule breaks the check; satisfying the check breaks the rule. There is no correct state. Same root as report-001's F-5, one file along: the gate does not read type, and neither does this check.

F-4Open

The candidate count partly measures corrections, not learning

Each correction becomes an observation, which becomes a candidate. Eight corrections clerked today became eight candidates. A meaningful share of the fifty-one is therefore the correction log one step along, and the count reads as how often the pioneer intervened rather than what the work taught.

More pointedly: all eight record a violation of a rule the skill already states. The rule about never leaving an id to stand alone was in the always-loaded file when it was broken, three times. They carry high certainty and contradict nothing — because the standard was already right and was not followed. A standard cannot learn from those. They are adherence evidence, filed where evidence about the standard goes.

F-5Verified

The learning diff is the only route by which a lesson learned once reaches the pioneer

A candidate is normally held until a sighting from a different reading arrives. A candidate born from a verified learning diff is due immediately, regardless of sighting count — because the diff is a second reading: the contract's prediction against what verification found.

Everything else waits for recurrence. Two of today's seven due candidates came this way, and they are the two that cost the most to learn. Worth knowing it is load-bearing — it is the difference between a standard that can absorb a hard lesson and one that must watch the lesson happen twice.

F-6Open

Twenty-three of twenty-four precedents are "not checkable"

Every precedent clerked from this project's corrections names a judgement — presentation clarity, sequencing, prioritisation, depth of understanding — and is marked as no fixed shape a script could verify. The casebook can instruct a future contract and cannot stop anything.

That may be inherent. It is worth stating because the practice's own line is that a check stops what it can and the human stops the rest — and in this project, as measured over two days, the human stops twelve of seventeen.

Who caught what

Five against twelve, over two days. The mechanisms caught everything with a shape and nothing without one, which is what they are for — and why the twelve on the right are the practice's actual load.

What this report does not establish

QuestionWhy it stayed open
What is the right turn budget? Not derived. F-1 shows the current ones fail and that cost tracks record size; a larger constant would fail later rather than differently.
Does the five-to-twelve ratio generalise? One project, two days, one agent, one pioneer. The categories are stable — formal versus judgement — but the proportion is a single sample.
Does the seal fail the same way elsewhere? It has failed once, at 0.24. The deny rule and the assembler's write scope are unchanged through 0.27 and no batch has been attempted since.
Is the blocked state correct in use? Never exercised. The digest made the audit possible, so nothing was recorded blocked.
Would the walk catch any of this? It passes 56 states and its fixture still carries no settings.json, no kit-sealed and no agents — so it cannot reach F-1, F-2 or F-3.
How much of the day's output is thin? Not audited. Report-004 documented three cases of reasoning outrunning reading; the same thinness may sit under work nobody questioned.

Recommendation — judgement, not finding

  1. Fix the seal. Unchanged through three releases and named in three reports. Fifty-one candidates, seven due, eleven drift entries and nine cards sit behind one write that cannot happen. Everything else here is secondary: the lifecycle now runs and has nowhere to put its output.
  2. Give the gate a notion of work in flight. One concept resolves all three symptoms in F-2. Without it the repetition detector will keep advising that completed work be recorded as failed, and collisions will keep being avoided only because someone is watching.
  3. Make agent budgets a function of what must be read — or make what must be read bounded, with an index or a working set. A constant cannot hold against a record that grows monotonically, and the failure mode is silence with the work done and unwritten.
  4. Reconcile G2-migration.sh with contract-018's rule. Either the check reads type, or the rule drops its exemption. Today no project can satisfy both.
  5. Consider whether corrections should become candidates where the correction is a breach of a rule the standard already states. Those are adherence evidence. Filing them as proposed standards inflates the queue the pioneer must clear with items that propose nothing.
  6. Consider where a communication check could sit. The kit already has one and it is post-hoc, inside an audit, which is why a person caught the same failure three times first. A check on what reaches the human has to sit between the writing and the sending, or it only ever tells them what they already know.

And one caution against acting on all of this. The instinct — the kit's and this agent's — is to convert every finding into a check. The five-to-twelve split says where that stops working. Twelve of seventeen failures had no shape a script could test, and a check built for them would measure a proxy that a fluent agent can satisfy while the thing itself stays thin. What worked was a person who knew the work well enough to ask the question with no comfortable answer. That capacity is the practice's real instrument, and it should be protected rather than automated.
